## One-liner

Northwind helps ops teams catch inventory problems before they hit the shelf,
without another dashboard to babysit.

---

## Voice & tone

- Confident, not hype. Every claim is one a customer could verify in a demo.
- Plain-spoken about a technical product. We explain, we don't posture.
- Curious and collaborative. We ask about the reader's problem before pitching.

---

## Value pillars

1. **Early warning** — surfaces stockout and overstock risk days ahead — *use for ops leaders feeling reactive.*
2. **No new busywork** — runs on data they already have — *use for skeptical, tool-fatigued buyers.*
3. **Trust the number** — reconciled against source systems — *use for finance-adjacent stakeholders.*

---

## Writing rules

- No em dashes. Use commas or periods.
- One clear CTA per message.
- Never mention pricing in outbound.
- CTAs are a question or a low-pressure offer, never a demand.
- Reference something specific about the reader's situation, not a generic hook.

---

## Banned language

**Openers**
- "Wanted to reach out"
- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "My name is X and I work at Y"

**CTAs**
- "Let me know if you're interested"
- "Circle back"
- "Jump on a quick call?"

**Subject lines**
- "Quick question"
- "Touching base"
- "Following up"

**Clichés / filler**
- "cutting-edge", "revolutionary", "game-changer", "synergy", "seamless"

---

## Approved phrases

- Say **"catch it before the shelf"** instead of "improve inventory accuracy"
- Say **"runs on the data you already have"** instead of "easy integration"
- Say **"worth a look?"** instead of "let me know if you'd like to chat"

---

## Channel conventions

- **LinkedIn** — no company name in the first line, lead with the reader's world.
- **Cold email** — subject under 5 words, body under 90 words, one CTA.
- **Follow-up** — add a new proof point or resource, never just "bumping this."
- **One-pager** — can run longer, still leads with the reader's problem.

---

## Red-flags checklist

- Is it about the reader, not about us?
- Is there exactly one clear CTA?
- Does it add value (not just "checking in")?
- Professional but human?
- Clean grammar, no banned language?
